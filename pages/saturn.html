Overview

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest in the Solar System. A gas giant like Jupiter, it's famous for its spectacular ring system — the most extensive and visible of any planet. Saturn has the lowest density of all planets (0.69 g/cm³), less than water, meaning it would float if placed in a large enough ocean.

Like Jupiter, Saturn is mostly hydrogen and helium with a rapid 10.7-hour rotation that flattens it at the poles. It radiates more heat than it receives from the Sun, powered by helium rain sinking through its interior.

The Rings

Saturn's rings extend up to 282,000 km from the planet but are remarkably thin — typically just 10-100 meters thick. Composed of >99% water ice (with traces of rock), they range from dust grains to house-sized chunks. The main rings (A, B, C) are separated by gaps like the Cassini Division (4,800 km wide), cleared by moon Mimas's gravity.

Shepherd moons (Pan, Daphnis, Atlas, Pandora, Prometheus) maintain sharp ring edges and create waves. The rings may be young (100-400 million years) — possibly debris from a shattered moon or comet — and are slowly raining material onto Saturn. They could vanish in ~100-300 million years.

Notable Moons

  • Titan — Largest moon, thick nitrogen atmosphere, methane/ethane lakes, organic chemistry
  • Enceladus — Icy crust, global subsurface ocean, geysers ejecting water into space (E-ring source)
  • Mimas — "Death Star" crater (Herschel), orbits within Cassini Division
  • Iapetus — Two-toned (bright/dark hemispheres), equatorial ridge
  • Rhea, Dione, Tethys — Mid-sized icy moons with varied cratering and tectonics

Titan and Enceladus are top targets in the search for extraterrestrial life.

Exploration

Pioneer 11 (1979), Voyager 1/2 (1980-81), and Cassini-Huygens (2004-2017) revolutionized Saturn science. Cassini orbited 294 times; its Huygens probe landed on Titan (2005) — the only outer Solar System landing. Cassini's Grand Finale dove between rings and planet before burning up. Dragonfly (NASA, launch 2028) will fly a rotorcraft on Titan in the 2030s.
