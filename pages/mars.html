Overview

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest in the Solar System. Known as the "Red Planet" due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface, Mars has a thin atmosphere composed mostly of carbon dioxide. It features the largest volcano (Olympus Mons), deepest canyon (Valles Marineris), and polar ice caps made of water and dry ice.

Mars has been a prime target for exploration due to its potential for past microbial life and future human settlement. Evidence suggests it once had liquid water on its surface billions of years ago.

Geography & Climate

Mars is a frozen desert with an average temperature of -60°C. The thin atmosphere (0.6% of Earth's pressure) cannot retain heat well. The northern hemisphere is smoother with younger plains, while the southern hemisphere is heavily cratered and older. Olympus Mons stands 21 km high — nearly three times the height of Mount Everest. Valles Marineris stretches over 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep.

Seasonal dust storms can engulf the entire planet. The polar caps grow and shrink with seasons, composed of water ice and frozen CO₂. Subsurface ice exists at mid-latitudes.

Exploration

Mars is the most explored planet beyond Earth. NASA's Mariner 4 (1965) returned the first close-up photos. The Viking landers (1976) conducted the first surface experiments. Since the 1990s, orbiters (Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, MAVEN) and rovers (Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance) have revolutionized our understanding.

Current missions search for signs of ancient life, study climate history, and test technologies for human exploration (like MOXIE producing oxygen from CO₂). Sample return missions are planned for the 2030s.

Moons

Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons: Phobos (22 km diameter) and Deimos (12 km). Both are likely captured asteroids. Phobos orbits just 6,000 km above Mars — closer than any other moon to its planet — and is gradually spiraling inward. In 30-50 million years, it may crash into Mars or break into a ring.
